Caregiver Stories

In their own words: caregivers share what Legacy and the Caregiver Sanctuary have meant to them.

Thank you for the chance to share how Legacy Home and Respite Care’s support has been priceless for me during this very difficult Alzheimer’s journey my father and our family are navigating.

Although we are about four years on our journey, and a month or two into this new stage requires me to handle more of his business and care. I felt so supported by Legacy’s founder and the resources she provided have truly been priceless.

— Lena C. Taylor

For me, the emotional toil of giving care to my Dad is the difficult part. The opportunity to specifically focus on my well being and self care is a godsend. Even when I am doing my best, I often feel overwhelmed and like I am failing.

The programming offered through Legacy gives caregivers permission and intention to engaging in self care. When Caregivers have a release, support and tools to care for themselves, the loved one who receives care benefits.

— Montez Ashley

The importance of the Caregiver Sanctuary held through Legacy Home and Respite Care

You don’t know what you don’t know until help arrives unexpectedly.

Nearly two years ago, I was introduced to a lifesaving supportive program. It is called the Legacy Home and Respite Care. What I didn’t know was that I was burning myself out and hadn’t taken time for me. I hadn’t realized that I have become so consumed with giving superlative care to my loved ones that I wasn’t taking time for me. Through Legacy Home and Respite Care, I discovered that even a few moments can make a difference. I have also found a way to take an hour or so for myself.

Read Lisa’s full story

Back story: I am a 24 hour caregiver. As I continue to age, I find that there are many individuals who need my personal help and support. My caregiving started with seniors in the area and grew to become my relatives and parents. My main focus and energy is now dedicated to my mother, my 89 year young community dad, and my 99 year young church dad. My Mother is the recipient of my 24 hour love and care.

Being such, I didn’t realize that working to provide care is more than dedication, it’s love but sometimes it goes beyond even what I thought.

I have learned a great deal with LHARC. First, I am able to leave my loved one with the organizers of the support group and take a little time for myself. Be it a short drive, self-care, or just a nap. This has truly made a difference in my personal challenges and has allowed me to complete some desperately year old actions.

Second, through LHARC, I was connected to a “Caregiving” class that expanded my knowledge about needs and services. More importantly, it taught me how to find resources, see caregiving through the eyes of those for whom we care, patterns of thinking, and the ultimate in terms of connections and lessons that prepare me for the upcoming and present needs.

The LHARC weekly sessions provide comfort, guidance, a space to vent, a space to get answers, a space to know “we are not alone”, and a space to learn from others. The bonds we build are strong. Even more, we are able to garner more knowledge in terms of strategies for care that prepare us for upcoming changes.

Furthermore, the Caregiver Sanctuary has helped me become comfortable with leaving my loved one in the hands of someone else. I am learning to let someone else take care of my mother. It is teaching me to not feel guilty about someone else caring for her and more importantly I feel very comfortable with our leaders having her. A little known secret is that I am more comfortable with Shanelle and Lauryn taking care of Mom than I am with some of my siblings. This is a blessing.

In all honesty, without this caregiver support group through Legacy I know that I would succumb to stress, frustration, and worry. I am more comfortable with accepting my loves at the space in time they are.

One last note, my cousin who is very close to me visited the most recent respite care day, she is most comfortable with our leaders and let me know that “I have to learn to let go and let them host.”

Thank you for taking the time to read this small portion of my gratitude for the Legacy Home and Respite Care’s “Caregiver Sanctuary.”

— Lisa Smith

I had always felt like God was punishing me for something I did not do though I knew God does not punish people. God allows things to happen to people to prepare them for another step he has planned and to help them grow.

I am the caregiver for my husband who was diagnosed with dementia. This is not a journey I would have chosen. I was brought up to not tell my business to people. My in-laws know what is happening with my husband as do my children and my oldest brother.

Read Joan’s full story

Recently my husband got lost driving a very familiar route he had travelled many times in the past. He called me at home and I was able to direct him home. We had a tracking app put on our phones. He had appts to go places that I now go with him. He still drives but I direct him as he should not be driving alone.

A few months ago a minister told me God would send me an angel. That angel seems to be my sister-in-law who has prayed with and for me. Despite my upbringing of not telling my business it helps to know I have support. There is a song “Encourage Yourself, Speak Over Yourself in the Lord”. Prayer has helped. Having my “angel” sister-in-law to talk to and pray with and for me helps. To know that I don’t have to go through this alone is a blessing and to have Legacy is a blessing.

My husband forgets a lot of things and he repeats a lot of things. I pray and sometimes cry. But my strength comes from God and the support of family and friends. Thank you.

— Joan Snowden

Legacy Home and Respite Care Foundation has been such a help to me and my family. If there is an event in the community to learn more about this disease and help better ourselves as caregivers, Legacy’s respite care team is always there making sure our loved ones are seen, safe, and loved.

My loved one was welcomed with open arms when they attended the memory social at the library. When I went to pick them up, they seemed very happy, and had a great time. They had created some art and danced. This is very important to get my loved one out of the house and active in the community. It’s also good for me to also have some time for myself. It is a great feeling to know that they are safe, and not have to worry.

Legacy has also created opportunities for caregivers to take care of themselves. I attended the massage event and it was such a great feeling to have 30 minutes to take care of myself and my health. Thank you Legacy!

— Shamiah Tanner
